## Optional generic build of the actual release

The archived portable tuning draft and the shipped release have different source. On the i7-12700H, two builds from the exact same release head `91fbc921` used the same compiler, release profile, packed relocations, Auto, one worker, and the same AVX2 CPU. The only intended codegen change was the compiler floor. Ten warmed alternating pairs per case gave the following paired changes in elapsed time. Each generic run was slower, and all four raw prefix hashes matched. Two independent observers linked to the exact release libraries confirmed that both the core resolver and the public decode context selected AVX2 at both compiler floors.

| Stream | Displayed frames | Generic versus shipped v3 [95% interval] |
|---|---:|---:|
| Noise Ocean | 16 | +173.16% [+162.82%, +179.80%] |
| RitualDance, 10-bit | 16 | +619.68% [+614.56%, +634.92%] |
| BlueSky | 64 | +535.32% [+518.00%, +542.70%] |
| OldTown, tiles 4×2 | 16 | +797.11% [+754.62%, +799.77%] |

The optional generic CLI measured 17,971,192 bytes and its C library 14,160,096 bytes. The matched v3 artifacts were 12,508,104 and 8,583,376 bytes. On the first four RitualDance frames Callgrind counted 8,363,079,041 guest instructions for generic and 1,917,583,024 for v3. A separately profiled symbolized generic link made 447,245,274 calls to generated Fearless SIMD primitive wrappers in those four frames. The matched symbolized v3 build recorded zero such calls. Primitive bodies accounted for 2.108 billion extra instructions, GDF functions another 2.458 billion. These instruction counts and sixteen-frame elapsed times refer to different windows. The symbolized links matched their exact local instruction totals within 2,098 instructions. `portable-release/` contains the build receipts, pairwise CPU/wall timings, raw hashes, and public backend observer. The separately tuned draft's +9.46% to +13.53% figures remain in the [original archive](../measurements.tar.xz).

## Results

- All 63 native streaming hash comparisons passed for the fully typed decoder on a Xeon 6975P-C using Auto, AVX2 and AVX-512 across seven frozen streams and three worker counts. Auto selected AVX-512 on this host. [Correctness and backend selection](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45674678371).
- In the first complete Xeon timing matrix, the AVX-512 grain row used 14.73% less elapsed time at 8 bits and 19.62% less at 10 bits. For the full grain stream, one-worker decode took 0.59% less time [−0.70%, −0.32%]. BlueSky at 12 workers took 1.45% more time [+0.14%, +2.43%]. The archive includes all 21 workload and worker combinations. [Initial matrix](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45670915732).
- Ten further pairs for typed grain output on native hardware gave −0.58%, −1.45% and −0.38% at 1, 7 and 12 workers. All three 95% intervals include zero. The two row medians were −14.14% and −18.54%. [Typed output measurements](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45673211431).
- The full typed Xeon matrix also recorded ordinary Noise at twelve workers at +0.80% [+0.09%, +1.52%] for forced AVX-512 versus forced AVX2, and OldTown at one worker at +0.09% [+0.01%, +0.23%]. The earlier BlueSky loss did not repeat in this matrix. The grain rows took 13.95% less time at 8 bits and 18.85% less at 10 bits. [Typed full matrix](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45674866900).
- On an independent Xeon Platinum 8573C the release binary ran the entire grain stream with forced AVX2 and forced AVX-512, alternating order for 48 warmed pairs at each worker count. AVX-512 took 0.56% less time at one worker [−0.86%, −0.29%], 0.53% less at seven [−1.18%, −0.27%] and 1.05% less at twelve [−1.42%, −0.35%]. [All 144 pairs](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45676645385).
- Twelve counterbalanced three-way rounds on one Xeon with four available vCPUs compared frozen, current main and the typed release using Auto. On the complete grain stream the candidate took 0.82% less time at one worker [−0.99%, −0.60%], 0.82% less at seven [−1.97%, +0.13%] and 1.21% less at twelve [−2.45%, −0.53%] versus main. All 21 full hash checks passed. OldTown at 7 and 12 workers was 0.29% and 0.53% slower than frozen, with positive intervals. Against current main those medians were −0.35% and +0.23%; both intervals included zero. [Three-way results](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45676519003).
- AVX2 Callgrind measurements of the typed candidate's 16-frame windows ranged from −0.968% to +0.104% against the frozen build and from −0.392% to −0.001% against main. OldTown64 took 3.06% longer [+1.10%, +5.09%] when comparing two experimental builds. The final candidate took 1.75% less elapsed time [−3.06%, −0.37%] against current main across 20 alternating blocks. The candidate also ran at a higher median clock. The archive preserves CPU frequency observations and individual samples for both comparisons where recorded.
- The hosted Apple M1 VM passed 42 complete per-revision AVM hash checks, native lifecycle tests and the C consumer. Four eight-pair timing intervals included zero. [Apple M1 comparison](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45670988686).
- The typed release also matched all 21 frozen native Apple M1 outputs at 1, 7 and 12 workers, passed four lifecycle tests, and passed the C sample via the measured dylib. The CLI grew 4,784 bytes and the dylib grew 16 bytes against the equivalent earlier parent build on a separate M1 VM. [Typed release Apple verification](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45677604248).
- The independent v3 Rust consumer grew 33,952 bytes (0.395%); the C host remained 14,544 bytes. The unlinked decoder rlib grew 10.8%. A single cold consumer build took 180.01 s on main and 184.44 s on the candidate, with peak command RSS rising from 2,695,096 to 3,083,972 KiB. All 12 Rust, C and CLI AVM comparisons passed.
- The final matched stripped CLI grew 43,328 bytes (+0.348%) and the C library grew 34,016 bytes (+0.398%). The linked audit covers 33 paths, the two wide grain leaves, code and data sections, and per-function stack reservations. [Exact artifact build](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45674741726).

- The final local v3 release built at 91fbc921 passed all 484 committed AVM decode-oracle fixture hashes. The stripped CLI was 12,508,104 bytes and the C library was 8,583,376 bytes on the workstation. The portable Rust API compilation reproducer and repository fast gate passed.
- The separate strict NEON subpel experiment removed all 18 feature-cache page references and all 18 initializer edges in the seven affected linked functions in both products. Sharing added 146,432 CLI bytes and 66,096 dylib bytes over the startup-only prototype. On hosted M1 the sharing candidate's first RitualDance picture was 9.79% faster than main [−13.97%, −2.36%]. BlueSky timing remained inconclusive. The experiment is outside the released decoder. The physical idle M2 Max and USDOT measurements remain open in [#1993](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/issues/1993#issuecomment-7170365932). [Three-way Apple comparison](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45674773435).

- On a further Xeon 6975P-C run with 48 pairs per worker count, the earlier non-grain signals did not repeat. At twelve workers, BlueSky was +0.04% [−1.02%, +0.65%] and ordinary Noise was +0.09% [−0.54%, +0.61%]. All six repeat intervals include zero. [All 288 non-grain pairs](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45680703061).
- The final release CI measured 13,007,490,453 decoder instructions across five windows against 13,007,493,851 on main. All five were within the per-stream and aggregate gates. Against the encoder's frozen thresholds, four cases used fewer instructions and 64×64 intra at preset zero used 0.031% more. Stripped CI artifacts measured 12,507,720 CLI bytes and 8,582,848 C library bytes. [CI performance job](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45680303776/job/139438972562).
- An independent, unshipped lookup-table experiment regressed the native eight-bit row by 298.61% [+295.40%, +299.63%]. The linked row called an outlined generic helper. The structural gate stopped that variant before whole-stream benchmarking. [Rejected optimization](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45677957869).

- The matched C libraries were measured for startup, first picture, p95 inter-picture delivery, drain, close and total time across 144 decode processes plus warm-ups. All first-picture, p95-delivery and drain intervals covered zero. The one-worker grained Noise total had a +3.46% paired median [−0.24%, +4.86%]. Sample timings and intervals are in `lifecycle/`. Three paired Valgrind heap checks found identical allocation counts and peak live bytes, with identical displayed pixels. Seven main/candidate call-trace pairs had equal out-of-line Arc last-owner counts.

- The final Linux static C consumer grew from 8,659,344 to 8,692,112 bytes (+0.378%). The unlinked static archive grew from 76,347,930 to 81,726,262 bytes (+7.045%), including substantial embedded compiler bitcode. Both eight- and ten-bit AVM fixtures and both longer prefix comparisons passed. The existing repository has no published Windows binary or native Windows CI coverage.
- A deliberate wrong-backend edge failed the saved linked-review procedure. The original CLI and shared C library had no resolved path from their audited AVX2 output roots to either wide leaf. The C deferred boundary selected the matching saved backend. The exercise records unresolved indirect edges and its scope in `codegen-negative/`.

- The production CLI and C library rebuilt from 91fbc921 match the archived c9a9a960 audited ELF products byte-for-byte after identical stripping. Their SHA-256s, compiler settings, and the single-policy control's matched sections are recorded in `isa-control/`. The dual release costs 47,008 CLI bytes and 31,824 library bytes over the single AVX-512 control at the same compiler floor. [Same-ISA control build](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45682290560).
- Exact-head native tests passed all 63 full AVM comparisons with Auto, AVX2 and AVX-512. The synchronous/streaming/deferred grain test and the independent random/tail scalar comparison both executed on the wide host. [Final native release oracle](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45680321894).

- Across 216 paired native comparisons of the final dual artifact against the single-wide control, no one of nine intervals was wholly above zero. On grained Noise, the dual artifact measured +0.01% [−0.27%, +0.20%], −0.16% [−0.81%, +0.39%] and −0.73% [−1.42%, +0.18%] at 1, 7 and 12 workers. The seven-worker BlueSky median was +1.37% [−0.51%, +1.91%]. Both binaries matched the complete AVM output for all seven streams. The archive includes all rows. The dual artifact executed safely on the AVX2 workstation. The matched single-wide control returned an unsupported-backend error before producing output on that machine. [Same-ISA timing](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45682290560).

- A corrected variant of the rejected lookup experiment, tracked in [#2007](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/issues/2007), kept the complete 64-pixel operation in the AVX-512 scope. On a native AMD EPYC 9V74 it reduced the 8-bit 1920-sample row time by 72.60% [−73.04%, −71.96%]. Whole 130-frame grained Noise playback improved 4.11% [−4.29%, −3.85%] at one worker and 7.34% [−8.30%, −6.90%] at seven. All four AVM inputs matched. The CLI grew another 1,600 bytes. These results belong to the isolated prototype. #2006 ships the earlier scaled-noise operation. [Prototype qualification](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/actions/runs/45682311244).

The earlier portable-floor experiments remain in the [original archive](../measurements.tar.xz). The [performance tracker](https://github.com/splotlabs/splot/issues/1934) links the outstanding work.
